Velobank is a licensed digital bank, and eligible deposits held in Velobank accounts are insured by the Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation (NDIC). This notice explains what that means for you, in plain language.

1. About this notice

This page is a summary of NDIC deposit insurance as it applies to Velobank accounts. It's intended to give you a general understanding of the protection in place for your funds — it doesn't replace official NDIC publications, which are the authoritative source on coverage limits and rules.

2. What is the NDIC

The Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation (NDIC) is a federal government agency that provides deposit insurance to depositors of licensed banks and other deposit-taking financial institutions in Nigeria. Its goal is to protect depositors and contribute to the stability of the financial system.

Insured institutions, including Velobank, pay premiums to the NDIC. These premiums fund the deposit insurance scheme — there's no direct cost to you as a depositor.

3. What's covered

Savings and current account balances held in your name at Velobank are eligible for NDIC deposit insurance, up to the maximum limit set by the NDIC, per depositor, per bank.

This means that if you hold both a savings and a current account with Velobank, your combined balance across both accounts is considered together when applying the coverage limit — not separately for each account.

4. Coverage limits

Deposit insurance coverage limits are set and periodically reviewed by the NDIC, in consultation with the Central Bank of Nigeria. Limits can change over time as policy is updated.

For the current, official coverage limit, always refer to the NDIC's own published information rather than relying solely on this page, as figures here may not reflect the most recent updates.

5. What's not covered

Deposit insurance covers eligible deposit balances only. It generally does not cover:

  • Investments, securities, or any non-deposit products
  • Funds held in accounts that aren't in your own name
  • Losses arising from fraud where you shared your PIN, password, or OTP with someone else

6. If Velobank were unable to meet its obligations

In the unlikely event that Velobank were unable to meet its obligations to depositors, the NDIC's role is to reimburse insured deposits up to the applicable limit, typically within a defined timeframe set out in NDIC procedures. You wouldn't need to take any action beyond following instructions issued by the NDIC at the time.

This is a standard protection that applies across licensed banks and deposit-taking institutions in Nigeria — it isn't unique to Velobank, and is one of the reasons deposit insurance exists.

7. Learn more

For the most current and authoritative information on deposit insurance coverage and limits, visit the official NDIC website. For questions about your specific Velobank account, reach out via the Contact page.
